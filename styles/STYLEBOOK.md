# Masteraiskill Style Atlas

A visual index for original, code-driven video design. Pick a style by the job the story needs to do—not by a trend. The names below are Masteraiskill presets; the sample concepts are illustrative directions, not claims that finished demos are included.

**How to use this atlas:** choose one lead style and, only when useful, one supporting style. Read its recipe, adapt the rules to the topic, then record the final palette, type, composition, materials, motion, captions, and audio in the project's style bible. Render an opening frame, a dense information frame, and a closing frame before building the full video.

## Style recipes

Each recipe defines a visual language, not a mandatory template. Follow the core rules, then adapt the palette, pacing, and visual metaphors to the story.

### Signal Ledger

**Look:** editorial grid, high-contrast type, disciplined margins, one accent color reserved for the key signal.  
**Motion:** reveal claim → show evidence → compare against baseline → resolve.  
**Rules:** show units and dates; label axes; let the audience read important numbers. Avoid decorative charts, arbitrary precision, and unsupported data.

### Margin Archive

**Look:** document fragments, page edges, margin annotations, source-aware footnotes.  
**Motion:** enter on the relevant line; highlight one detail; connect it to the next consequence.  
**Rules:** distinguish genuine source material from recreated graphics. Do not invent a quotation, document, signature, or archival artifact.

### Casefile Motion

**Look:** evidence cards, clue paths, restrained stamps, linked timestamps.  
**Motion:** surface one clue, test its relevance, then connect it to the larger explanation.  
**Rules:** preserve the difference between evidence, inference, and speculation. Avoid implying wrongdoing or certainty that sources do not establish.

### Timecode Atlas

**Look:** date rails, map fragments, chapter markers, contextual captions.  
**Motion:** anchor the viewer in time before moving to the next event; use recurring markers for continuity.  
**Rules:** make chronology explicit and show when events overlap. Do not compress time in a way that misrepresents causality.

### Groundtruth Grid

**Look:** measured maps, route lines, geographic labels, minimal map furniture.  
**Motion:** trace a route or expand the map only when geography matters to the explanation.  
**Rules:** use verified geographic information, show the scale when necessary, and do not place invented routes on a real map.

### Broadcast Pulse

**Look:** headline band, lower thirds, headline-sized data, short source labels.  
**Motion:** fast but readable transitions; reserve urgent movement for truly time-sensitive information.  
**Rules:** label the date of current information and avoid fabricated live tickers or simulated authority.

### Foundry Notes

**Look:** component outlines, measurements, numbered callouts, engineering-note typography.  
**Motion:** isolate the part under discussion, reveal its function, then reconnect it to the whole.  
**Rules:** align callouts to the actual component and keep units legible. Avoid pretending an illustrative diagram is a verified technical drawing.

### Paper Signal

**Look:** flat 2D paper cards, imperfect cut edges, subtle fibers, layered shadows, original cutout objects.  
**Motion:** slide, stack, peel, fold-like transitions, restrained parallax.  
**Rules:** remain 2D; keep a stable paper palette; use texture sparingly. Avoid plastic-looking bevels, random floating scraps, and depth effects that overwhelm the idea.

### Cutshape Theatre

**Look:** large flat silhouettes, geometric props, staged negative space, deliberate color blocking.  
**Motion:** compose like a scene on a small theatre stage; use blocking and scale to show power, contrast, or cause and effect.  
**Rules:** silhouettes must be readable at final video size. Avoid introducing decorative objects without a story role.

### Inkline Journey

**Look:** precise ink contours, sparse wash colors, directional line work.  
**Motion:** let a path, outline, or route lead the eye through the frame.  
**Rules:** preserve contrast and line weight at mobile size. Do not fake a hand-drawn process with strokes that miss the actual object.

### Chalkthread Lab

**Look:** board-like negative space, chalk lines, formula fragments, one or two highlight colors.  
**Motion:** draw the reasoning in order; hold the completed equation or diagram before moving on.  
**Rules:** keep formulas correct and handwriting short. Avoid covering the explanation with excessive chalk dust or illegible notation.

### Scribble Circuit

**Look:** deliberately loose doodles, simple arrows, hand-lettered accents and a small recurring symbol set.  
**Motion:** doodles should connect steps or resolve into a clean diagram.  
**Rules:** maintain one consistent pen language. The handmade quality should feel authored, not randomly jittery.

### Storybook Drift

**Look:** illustrated environments, soft-edged shapes, purposeful page-like framing.  
**Motion:** move between visual chapters with a page shift, reveal, or change of scale.  
**Rules:** use original scenes and a coherent palette. Keep narration and visual metaphors simple enough for a broad audience.

### Collage Current

**Look:** layered flat shapes, cropped print textures, paper fragments and tightly controlled contrast.  
**Motion:** assemble a collage as ideas accumulate; remove layers when simplifying.  
**Rules:** use assets with a clear provenance and maintain a consistent material system. Avoid a chaotic mix of unrelated cutouts.

### Number Stage

**Look:** a dominant number, its unit, a comparison point and a clear context label.  
**Motion:** count only when counting helps; stage the numerator and denominator before revealing a ratio.  
**Rules:** derive values from recorded sources or data files. Do not use animation to imply precision the source does not support.

### Causal Loom

**Look:** nodes, arrows, labels and links forming a readable cause-and-effect system.  
**Motion:** reveal each dependency in explanatory order, then show the combined result.  
**Rules:** arrow direction and link meaning must be explicit. Differentiate direct causation from correlation, assumptions, and feedback loops.

### Flowline Atlas

**Look:** paths or bands whose width and direction represent movement through a system.  
**Motion:** follow the item as it transfers between stages; keep the destination visible.  
**Rules:** make clear whether flow width is proportional or merely illustrative. Avoid changing quantities midstream without explaining losses, conversion, or units.

### Market Mechanics

**Look:** aligned price cards, demand/supply panels, margin bars and comparison anchors.  
**Motion:** change one variable at a time before showing the market outcome.  
**Rules:** label assumptions, time periods and units. Do not treat an illustrative model as a measured real-world result.

### Scale Ladder

**Look:** repeated visual object shown at clearly labeled scales or magnitudes.  
**Motion:** zoom or step between levels while preserving a visual anchor.  
**Rules:** label scale changes, ratios and units. Avoid transitions that make incomparable sizes appear quantitatively exact.

### Branching Logic

**Look:** clean branches, decision nodes, path states and outcome labels.  
**Motion:** illuminate the chosen branch, then reveal alternatives when the comparison matters.  
**Rules:** make each decision condition understandable. Do not hide rejected options when their omission changes the meaning of the explanation.

### Type Pulse

**Look:** bold typography, controlled line breaks, strong contrast, one main message per beat.  
**Motion:** use scale, position, masking or kinetic emphasis to deliver meaning—not arbitrary bouncing.  
**Rules:** keep text brief, readable, and inside platform-safe areas. Never replace spoken explanation with a wall of on-screen text.

### Screenlane

**Look:** real or accurately recreated interface panels, clear zoom targets and cursor paths.  
**Motion:** show a complete action, pause on the outcome, and annotate only the detail the audience needs.  
**Rules:** do not fabricate real product UI or imply an unverified feature exists. Obscure private or sensitive data before using screenshots.

### Glyph Reactor

**Look:** letters and numbers transform into simple abstract shapes or diagrams.  
**Motion:** transitions begin with a readable word, transform, and end on a new legible state.  
**Rules:** the transformation must preserve meaning and stay short enough not to become a puzzle.

### Interface Theatre

**Look:** staged interface panels, focus rings, callouts, progress steps and benefit-led captions.  
**Motion:** problem → interaction → visible result.  
**Rules:** demonstrate real behavior when possible. Mark conceptual screens as illustrations and avoid fake analytics or user records presented as real.

### Terminal Bloom

**Look:** monospace typography, log strips, restrained luminous accents, dark negative space.  
**Motion:** lines appear as meaningful events; traces resolve into a diagram or output.  
**Rules:** code and logs must be plausible and legible. Do not present fabricated logs as actual output from a real system.

### Isometric Foundry

**Look:** one consistent isometric projection, layered modules, limited face shading and stable geometry.  
**Motion:** construct from the foundation upward or separate components along consistent axes.  
**Rules:** do not switch projection conventions mid-sequence. Label hidden processes and keep front/back relationships understandable.

### Cross-Section Studio

**Look:** cutaway layers, material blocks, numbered components and direct labels.  
**Motion:** remove or separate only the layer currently being explained; restore context before advancing.  
**Rules:** source technical details and distinguish conceptual cutaways from exact engineering schematics.

### Orbit Assembly

**Look:** a strong central object or concept surrounded by components, inputs, or dependencies.  
**Motion:** bring in each component with a clear relationship to the center, then show the whole system.  
**Rules:** orbiting is not automatically informative. Keep labels fixed or readable, and avoid spin that disrupts orientation.

### Miniature Field

**Look:** compact diorama-like scenes, simplified environments, measured depth and high-contrast focal objects.  
**Motion:** subtle camera drift, staged reveals and scale changes that support context.  
**Rules:** keep the scene readable when reduced to phone size. Use miniature depth only where it helps the story.

### Shadow Cabinet

**Look:** bold silhouettes, small pools of light, partial reveals and strong contrast.  
**Motion:** reveal the outline before the detail, then widen the frame as the explanation becomes clearer.  
**Rules:** do not use cinematic mystery to imply facts that are unproven. Keep text separate from shadowed visual detail.

### Rubberline Cast

**Look:** simple reusable characters, expressive poses, consistent outlines and clear props.  
**Motion:** gesture, reaction and object handoff should communicate the beat even with audio muted.  
**Rules:** keep character scale, anatomy and identity consistent from scene to scene. Avoid unnecessary lip-sync when narration is not character dialogue.

### Cel Memory

**Look:** flat painted layers, clean outlines, restrained background detail and frame-like scene changes.  
**Motion:** pose changes, lateral pans and scene transitions with a consistent animation rhythm.  
**Rules:** keep the character and setting designs consistent. Use sourced references responsibly and avoid copying a specific living artist's signature style.

### Silhouette Folio

**Look:** layered dark figures, symbolic objects, paper-like scenery and controlled lighting.  
**Motion:** staged blocking, shadow reveals and deliberate transitions between symbolic spaces.  
**Rules:** every silhouette needs a recognizable purpose. Keep symbolic scenes distinguishable from documentary evidence.

### Signal Bloom

**Look:** procedural lines, fields, waves and restrained glow around a clear focal point.  
**Motion:** animate from frame/time inputs; seed randomness so renders are repeatable.  
**Rules:** effects must expose a pattern or relationship. Avoid particle soup, uncontrolled flicker and costly effects that add no explanatory value.

### Particle Grammar

**Look:** particles follow a visible organizing rule: cluster, spread, align, split or converge.  
**Motion:** show the rule first, then let the system evolve into the meaningful form.  
**Rules:** control particle count and deterministic seeds. Never imply that an artistic simulation is a scientifically validated model.

### Microcosm Engine

**Look:** close-up abstract structures, layered cells or components, tightly framed detail.  
**Motion:** move between states with a clear before/after relationship and a stable reference point.  
**Rules:** distinguish schematic imagery from microscopy or real scientific footage. Keep colors and labels meaningful rather than decorative.

### Depth Signal

**Look:** layered planes, controlled parallax, sparse geometry and clean light gradients.  
**Motion:** depth shifts support chapter transitions or conceptual scale changes rather than constant camera movement.  
**Rules:** maintain a primary focal point and readable type. Avoid using depth as a substitute for a real visual argument.

## Combining styles

- Select **one primary style** for identity and one secondary style only when it performs a different job.
- Record which style applies to each sequence in the storyboard. Do not switch randomly between visual systems.
- Reuse a shared palette and typography, even when a sequence uses a different rendering technique.
- Keep data-backed graphics truthful and label illustrative values or conceptual diagrams.
- Render and inspect the opening, information-dense middle, and final frame at delivery size.
- If a style cannot carry an idea clearly, change the visual metaphor—not just the amount of decoration.

## Choosing a style quickly

| Story need | Good starting point |
|---|---|
| Explain money, margins, pricing, or economic cause and effect | Signal Ledger, Market Mechanics, Causal Loom |
| Tell a company or historical story | Timecode Atlas, Margin Archive, Foundry Notes |
| Explain geography, logistics, or infrastructure | Groundtruth Grid, Flowline Atlas, Isometric Foundry |
| Create a tactile 2D handmade explainer | Paper Signal, Cutshape Theatre, Collage Current |
| Teach a sequence or formula | Chalkthread Lab, Causal Loom, Branching Logic |
| Explain software or an app | Screenlane, Interface Theatre, Terminal Bloom |
| Explain objects or internal mechanisms | Cross-Section Studio, Orbit Assembly, Foundry Notes |
| Make an idea feel cinematic without relying on stock footage | Shadow Cabinet, Signal Bloom, Depth Signal |
| Create a character-led story | Rubberline Cast, Storybook Drift, Cel Memory |
| Make a high-impact short-form opening | Type Pulse, Number Stage, Broadcast Pulse |
