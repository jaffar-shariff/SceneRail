# Long-form Explainer

Use for multi-minute explainers, business/history breakdowns, technology explainers, documentary essays, and narrated editorial videos.

## Process
1. State the thesis and what the viewer currently believes, wonders, or misunderstands.
2. Research important claims. Record source, date when relevant, evidence summary, and uncertainty.
3. Divide the story into chapters that develop the idea in a logical progression. Each chapter should raise or answer a real question and leave the viewer understanding something new.
4. Write spoken-language narration before detailed visuals. Use clear causal links, concrete examples, meaningful contrasts, and varied sentence rhythm. Avoid filler, endless rhetorical questions, and claims without evidence.
5. Estimate duration from word count and pace, then retime the script after narration is recorded/generated.
6. Create a beat map: one main idea, visual purpose, narration range, expected duration, and emotional/intellectual shift per beat.
7. Map each beat to a scene and transition. Ensure the visuals explain the spoken point rather than merely decorate it.
8. Review chapter flow, callbacks, counterpoints, escalation, and whether the ending fulfils the opening promise.

## Long-form tests
- Can the thesis be said in one sentence?
- Does every chapter add meaning rather than restate earlier points?
- Are key mechanisms demonstrated rather than merely asserted?
- Are important numbers, dates, quotes, and claims sourced?
- Does the pace vary intentionally, with enough hold time for complex information?
- Does the ending pay off the opening?
- Is the target runtime achieved through substance rather than padding?

## Outputs
- `script.md`: narration grouped by chapter and beat.
- `sources.json`: claim/source ledger.
- `beats.json`: beat ID, intent, narration range, expected duration, visual purpose.
- `storyboard.json`: scene plans linked to beats.
